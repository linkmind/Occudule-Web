Google is bringing Gemini into Gmail. It can summarize threads, pull details into Google Calendar, and take other actions inside the mailbox you already use. On the surface, that can look a lot like **Occudule**.

The overlap is real: both try to save parents time when school mail piles up. The jobs are not the same. Gemini is a general assistant sitting on a Google account. Occudule is a school-and-family logistics app. It works with **Gmail and Microsoft** email (Outlook, Hotmail, Live, and MSN), it only processes messages that match the criteria you set, and it stores structured **Events** and **Info**—not a second copy of your inbox.

If you have been wondering whether Gemini in Gmail replaces a tool like Occudule, here is how the two actually differ.

## A general inbox AI vs a school-email specialist

Gemini in Gmail is built for *email in general*. It can help with work threads, shopping receipts, newsletters, and school notes in the same place. To do that, you give Gemini access to your Google account. For many parents, that is the pause: a capable AI with a wide view of the mailbox, acting in ways that are easy to miss.

Occudule is built for one domain: **kids’ schools and after-school programs**. Permission slips, pizza days, sports schedules, club notices, and “please sign up by Friday” messages are the workload it was designed around. It is not trying to be a general assistant for every message you receive. That focus is why Occudule can do things a general model in Gmail is not set up to do well—starting with how it decides which emails even matter.

## How Occudule decides what to process

Occudule does not roam your inbox looking for anything it might find interesting. You tell it who your children are and where they go.

On **Family Profile**, you add each child, their grade, school name and email domain, other education institutions (clubs, tutoring, teams), and teachers’ names. Those details become the matching rules.

When mail arrives, Occudule scores it against those rules:

- Does the sender’s domain match a school or institution you listed?
- Is the sender on the specific email addresses you added (useful when a coach or teacher writes from a personal Gmail)?
- Do the subject or body mention your child’s name, grade, school, or a teacher you entered?
- Do built-in school keywords (field trip, permission, sign-up, and similar) appear?

High-confidence matches go through Occudule’s processing pipeline. Borderline messages can be shown for your review. Mail that does not look school-related is left alone.

That is the practical answer to “will AI do something behind me?” Occudule is not rewriting, deleting, or replying to the rest of your inbox. It is looking for *targeted* school mail, then extracting key details from those messages only.

## What Occudule stores (and what it does not)

For a matched school email, Occudule still does not keep the full message as your system of record. It **extracts the pieces you need**—who it is for, what it is about, when it happens, what you must do, and the links that matter—and stores those structured fields in the app.

Your other mail stays in Gmail or Microsoft. Occudule does not delete it, file it, or send replies on your behalf. Optional draft-reply help, where it exists, stays under your control: you review and send.

That is a different privacy posture from handing a general assistant the run of a Google account. Occudule is scoped to the school-mail job you configured. See our [Privacy Policy](/privacy) for how connected and forwarded mail is handled.

## Gmail *and* Microsoft, not Google only

Gemini in Gmail only helps if your school mail lives in Gmail. Plenty of families use **Outlook, Hotmail, Live, or MSN**. Occudule is built for both.

- **Microsoft users:** Connect the mailbox once. When a message from your child’s school or program hits that inbox, Occudule can detect it, process it, extract the key details, categorize it, and store it in the app. You do not have to hunt the inbox first.
- **Gmail users:** Forward school-related messages to your Occudule address (saved as **Occudule Forward** in Google Contacts). You choose which threads leave Gmail. Occudule then runs the same filtering and extraction pipeline.

Sign-in can be Google, Microsoft, Apple, or email and password. Mail sync is always a Gmail or Microsoft mailbox. ## Two record types: Event and Info

A general summary of “this email is about a field trip” is useful. Parents still need two different kinds of follow-up. Occudule splits school mail into **Event** and **Info** on purpose.

**Events** are time-sensitive. Occudule reminds you what the activity is, gives a brief summary, captures deadlines, turns action items into **to-dos**, and keeps the links you need—so a school sign-up can be one tap instead of a scavenger hunt through last week’s thread.

**Info** is what the school wants parents to know when there is no calendar item: newsletters, reminders, policy notes, “please be aware” messages. You do not have to read every paragraph. Occudule extracts the key points for *your* children (and whole-school notices), keeps related links for reference, and skips saving cards that are only about other grades or programs.

Those two types serve different jobs. Events drive the calendar and to-dos. Info is the reference shelf. Together they are easier to scan than a long Gmail thread—or a generic AI recap that still lives inside the inbox.

## Everything about your kids, in one structured place

The hidden cost of school email is not only reading it. It is finding it again: the permission link from Tuesday, the early-dismissal note, the club fee, the other child’s concert time.

Occudule stores those activities and notices **in one app, by child, in a structured layout**. You are not scrolling a mixed inbox hoping the right subject line still looks familiar. You open Occudule and review Events, Info, and to-dos that already belong to your family.

That is also why Occudule is a **domain expert** in this slice of life, rather than a general model bolted onto email. It knows to look for the child, the school, the deadline, the sign-up link, and whether this is a date on the calendar or a note to keep. Gemini in Gmail can be excellent at many inbox tasks. It is not a family hub purpose-built for school and after-school logistics across Gmail *and* Microsoft.
